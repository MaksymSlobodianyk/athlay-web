Installing Athlay

Athlay isn’t on the App Store yet. Until it is, you get it through TestFlight. It takes about two minutes.

✓

TestFlight is made by Apple. It is the normal, official way to try an iPhone app before it reaches the App Store, and you download it from the App Store like any other app.

What TestFlight is

If you haven’t used it before, the invite can look odd. So, plainly: TestFlight is Apple’s own app. Apple built it, Apple runs it, and it’s what nearly every iPhone app you already use went through before it launched.

A few things worth knowing:

  • You install TestFlight from the App Store, the same as any other app. Nothing is sideloaded and nothing is jailbroken.
  • Apple reviews the app builds that go out through TestFlight.
  • You sign in with the Apple ID you already have. There is no new account and no password to create for us.
  • The invite email is sent by Apple, not by us. The sender address ends in @email.apple.com.
  • It’s free. TestFlight costs nothing and the Athlay beta costs nothing.

You can read Apple’s own description on the TestFlight page, and see the app itself on the App Store.

Before you start

  • An iPhone running iOS 26 or later. Athlay is iPhone-only for now. It won’t install on an iPad.
  • The invite email from TestFlight. We send it to the address you gave us. If it’s not in your inbox, check spam and promotions.

Not sure which version you’re on? Open Settings → General → About and look at Software Version. Anything below 26, update from Settings → General → Software Update.

Step by step

  1. Open the invite email

    On your iPhone, open the email titled “Maksym Slobodianyk has invited you to test Athlay.” It comes from TestFlight.

  2. Get TestFlight from the App Store

    If TestFlight isn’t on your phone yet, install it first. On your iPhone, open TestFlight on the App Store and tap Get — or search the App Store for TestFlight. It’s the blue icon with the white propeller, published by Apple.

    Already have it? Skip to the next step.

  3. Install Athlay

    Back in the email, tap the blue View in TestFlight button. TestFlight opens on Athlay’s page. Tap Install.

    You’ll see the version number, the build number and how many days are left before that build expires.

  4. Open it

    Athlay is now on your Home Screen, like any other app. Tap it to open — from there or from TestFlight, it makes no difference.

  5. Check out what’s new in this version

    The first time you open a new build, you’ll see a screen headed From the Developer, listing what changed since the last one. It comes from TestFlight, not from inside Athlay.

    Read it if you like, tap Continue, and you’re in the app. You’ll see this again each time you update to a newer build.

Telling us what’s broken

This is a beta, so things will be rough in places. That’s the point — we’d rather hear about it than not.

The quickest way: take a screenshot inside Athlay, then tap the preview in the corner and choose Share Beta Feedback. Your note and the screenshot come straight to us.

You can also open Athlay’s page in TestFlight and tap Send Feedback, or just email support@athlay.app. Whatever’s easiest.

Good to know

  • You can leave whenever. Delete the app the way you’d delete any other, or tap Stop Testing in TestFlight.
  • When Athlay launches properly, you install the App Store version and delete the beta.
